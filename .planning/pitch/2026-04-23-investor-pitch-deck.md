## Slide 2 — The Problem

# Dating is broken. Everyone knows it.

- **73% of Gen Z** say dating apps feel like a second job. *(Pew, 2024)*
- **59% of users** quit apps within 90 days. *(Hinge internal, cited in TechCrunch 2024)*
- Average user: **7 matches**, **1.3 actual dates**, **0.2 second dates**.
- The industry's own CEOs call it "swipe fatigue."

## What actually happens:

1. Swipe hundreds of profiles based on photos
2. Match with a few
3. Small-talk in-app for days
4. *Maybe* exchange numbers
5. *Maybe* go on a date — usually a low-effort drink
6. Disappointed. Delete. Reinstall in a week.

**The unit of attraction is wrong.** People aren't profiles.

> *The stats aren't cherry-picked — dating app churn is brutal and openly documented. Match Group's own 10-K calls out subscriber decline. This slide builds common ground — investor nods. Don't fight for attention here; let the data do it.*

## Slide 3 — The Insight

# The first date is the product. Not the profile.

Every research study on long-term relationships says the same thing:

Yet every dating app optimizes for the part *before* context.

**What if the app matched you on the experience you'd share — not the face you'd judge?**

## Slide 4 — The Solution

# After5

**The AI plans the date. You pick who to share it with.**

1. **Lucas opens After5.** Tells it his budget, vibe, the evening ahead.
2. **AI generates three complete date plans** — real venues, real timing, real local knowledge. Pinterest-quality photos.
3. **Lucas picks his favorite. Taps "find match."**
4. **The date enters a public swipe queue.** Others see the date — not Lucas's profile — and swipe.
5. **Lucas sees who swiped. Picks his favorite.** Mutual right-swipe = match.
6. **They meet up. They rate the date afterward.** The system gets smarter.

**No endless chat. No catfishing. No "dead profiles."**
**Just: a good plan + a real person = a real date.**

> *This is the "aha" walkthrough. Actually screen-share the product if you can — the flow is visual. Emphasize the reversal: most apps ask "who do you want to meet?" After5 asks "what do you want to do tonight?" — the person follows. Power dynamic is different. Safer-feeling. More intent.*

## Slide 5 — Product (How it works)

# Three products in one app, compounding.

### 1. AI Date Generator
Generates 3 custom date plans in 12 seconds. Real venues, real hours, real photos. Hallucination-proof by design — the LLM writes voice, never picks places.

### 2. Swipe-on-Dates Feed
The unique mechanic. Every generated date can optionally enter the swipe queue. Filters: age, payment preference (he pays / she pays / 50-50), distance, vibe.

### 3. Venue Partnership Graph
Every venue in every city can claim their profile, offer After5-exclusive discounts, and show up in more plans. Partners drive traffic; users get deals; we get verified data.

**Flywheel:**
```
Better plans → more users → more dates generated →
richer content → more venue partners → better plans →
  …more users.
```

> *This slide exists so investors understand it's not just a dating app — it's a three-sided marketplace (users × venues × content). Each side reinforces the others. This is defensible.*

## Slide 8 — Competition

# Nobody does swipe-on-plans. Here's why it's defensible.

|  | Profile-first dating | Editorial experiences | Bookings | Swipe-on-plans |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tinder / Hinge / Bumble | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ |
| The Infatuation / Atlas Obscura | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| Resy / OpenTable | ❌ | ⚠️ Light | ✅ | ❌ |
| TimeOut | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **After5** | ⚠️ Secondary | ✅ | 🔜 via partnerships | ✅ |

**What makes this defensible:**

1. **Data flywheel** — each rating on a completed date teaches the system which venues work for which kinds of dates. Incumbents can't get this without changing their product shape.
2. **Venue relationships** — our outreach pipeline converts venues into exclusive-discount partners. A classic dating app has no reason to build this graph.
3. **Plan-as-content** — every generated date is a piece of social content. We post on TikTok/Reels/Shorts automatically. Dating apps don't produce content; content platforms don't produce matches.

**Match Group can't buy this. They'd have to build a parallel business.**

> *Competition slides usually backfire when founders trash-talk incumbents. Don't. Acknowledge Tinder/Bumble's strengths, then show why our shape is structurally different, not just "better."*

## Slide 9 — Business Model

# Three revenue streams, progressively unlocked.

### Phase 1 — Partner revenue *(Month 4+)*
- Venues pay a **featured listing fee** ($49–$299/mo) to appear more frequently in generated plans
- Verified partners offer **After5-exclusive discount codes** — we take a small affiliate cut
- *Projected:* $8k-$20k MRR by end of Year 1 in Kelowna alone

### Phase 2 — User subscriptions *(Month 8+)*
- **Free forever:** generate dates, swipe, match (first 1,000 signups free, expandable)
- **After5 Plus ($9/mo):** priority in match queues, unlimited find-match, advanced filters, see who swiped you
- Industry benchmark: 4–8% paid conversion on dating apps

### Phase 3 — Affiliate & ads *(Year 2+)*
- Resy / OpenTable affiliate payouts when bookings happen through us
- Sponsored city content ("This week on After5 Toronto, sponsored by…")
- Event ticketing affiliate (Ticketmaster, SeatGeek)

**Projected Year 2 ARR at 5 cities: $2.1M–$3.4M.**

## Slide 12 — Go-to-Market

# How we launch each city.

### The playbook (per new city, ~6 week cycle)

**Week -4 through 0:**
1. Bulk-seed venue database from Overture + Foursquare OS (free, ~10k–40k POIs per metro)
2. Reach out to city tourism board — they *want* to share their curated best-of lists
3. Hire 1 local part-time curator ($20–40/hr, 10hrs/wk) — trims the bulk list to 200–500 quality venues

**Launch week:**
4. Top 50 "hit list" manually editorialized with cover imagery
5. Top 200 venues enriched via Google Places (hours, photos, rating)

**Post-launch (weeks 1–12):**
6. Outreach pipeline sends 100/week AI-drafted contact emails → converts venues to partners → discount codes accumulate
7. Social content pipeline auto-posts 3–5 videos/week to TikTok/Reels/Shorts for the city
8. Word-of-mouth among early users (Kelowna has 30+ user-to-user invites per week)

### Target: 10 cities in Canada + US by end of Year 2
Vancouver → Calgary → Toronto → Seattle → Portland → Denver → Austin → Nashville → Brooklyn → Chicago

> *The city-by-city playbook is a real moat — competitors can't replicate it overnight. Emphasize the automation: 80% of the per-city launch work runs itself (ingestion, outreach, social). Only curation stays human.*

# Speaker notes — pitch delivery tips

**Opening (Slides 1–4):** Slow. Confident. The concept is the whole game — if they don't get "swipe on dates, not people" in the first 90 seconds, nothing else lands. Test your 30-second pitch before investor meetings.

**Middle (Slides 5–11):** This is where you answer the "will this work?" question. Be specific. Investors are pattern-matching against companies they've seen before — Atlas Obscura × Hinge × Resy is not a pattern most have seen. Take the time.

**Close (Slides 12–15):** Shift to "why you, why now." Vision + team + ask. Clean, direct, no hedging on the number.

**Q&A prep — questions you will be asked:**
1. "What's stopping Match Group from copying this in 6 months?" → Data flywheel + venue relationships + built-in content pipeline. They'd have to rebuild their product shape.
2. "Dating apps are brutal unit economics. Why will you be different?" → Organic acquisition via automated content. Partner revenue before user revenue. Honest about the bet.
3. "What's your moat if a well-funded competitor enters?" → 6-month head start on city expansion + venue partnerships + content inventory. All compounding.
4. "Solo founder — why haven't you raised before?" → Built to MVP before raising. Now scaling requires capital. Clean story.
5. "What if you can't get App Store approval?" → Moderation design is dating-app-appropriate from day one. Phased rollout means we learn on web before mobile review.
6. "Why Kelowna?" → Founder-market fit. Smaller city = cleaner testbed. Playbook transfers to bigger cities without changes.
7. "Have you talked to users?" → [BE READY — have 5+ specific user stories memorized, not just screenshots]

**Red flags to preempt:**
- Dating app reputation (harassment, fake profiles): surface moderation strategy EARLY, don't wait for them to raise it
- "Solo founder" concern: name the advisor + hire plan on Slide 14
- "AI bubble" skepticism: position AI as enabling infrastructure, not the product itself
